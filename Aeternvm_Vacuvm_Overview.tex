\begin{abstract}
We present a coherent theoretical framework (Aeternvm Vacuvm) that reinterprets the present $\sim3\sigma$ first-row CKM unitarity deficit as a signature of a depletable vacuum rather than new particles. Three companion papers develop the idea: (I) continuum extrapolation and parameter scan fixing $\kappa_{\rm vac}\simeq5\times10^{-4}$; (II) predicted $A$-dependence of $V_{ud}$ from superallowed decays; (III) a working toy lattice implementation with dynamical depletion field. All numerical results are illustrative; full-scale Lattice QCD validation remains future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
Lattice QCD evaluations of the $\gamma W$-box correction have reduced the theoretical uncertainty on $V_{ud}$ extracted from superallowed $\beta$ decays and revealed a deficit
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm CKM}=1-(|V_{ud}|^2+|V_{us}|^2+|V_{ub}|^2)\simeq0.0015(5).
\end{equation}
We explore whether this deficit can be understood as the work required to deform a dynamical vacuum density $\phi$.

\section{Core Hypothesis}
The vacuum is assigned a local density field $\phi$ with potential $V(\phi)=\lambda(\phi^2-v^2)^2$. Coupling to the gauge sector via
\begin{equation}
S\supset\kappa_{\rm vac}\,\phi\,{\rm Tr}(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})
\end{equation}
induces an effective reduction of the weak matrix element
\begin{equation}
V_{ud}^{\rm eff}=V_{ud}^{\rm bare}(1-\kappa_{\rm vac}\langle\phi\rangle).
\end{equation}
A single ultra-weak coupling $\kappa_{\rm vac}\sim5\times10^{-4}$ accounts for the entire observed deficit.

\section{Summary of the Trilogy}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Paper I} performs a parameter scan and continuum extrapolation showing that the deficit survives $a\to0$.
\item \textbf{Paper II} predicts and fits a linear decrease of $V_{ud}$ with nuclear mass number $A$.
\item \textbf{Paper III} supplies a minimal Metropolis code on $16^4$ that realizes the depletion mechanism and reproduces the target deficit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook}
The framework yields concrete, falsifiable predictions (ratio $R=V_{ud}(^{54}{\rm Co})/V_{ud}(^{10}{\rm C})$, magnetic-field dependence of $\Delta_R^V$, free-neutron versus nuclear tension). These can be tested with existing or near-future experimental precision without requiring new particles.
